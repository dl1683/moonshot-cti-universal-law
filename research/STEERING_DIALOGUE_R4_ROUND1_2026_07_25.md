## Decision

**No: DG-0, as currently specified, should no longer be the primary direction. Do not run the six-arm Qwen/GSM8K pilot.**

The defensive analysis did not merely make DG-0 expensive. It revealed that the proposed primitive—raw update-to-state/skew geometry—has not earned privileged status:

- It is non-identifiable, gauge-sensitive, batch-dependent, and can live in an output-ignored sidecar.
- A positive Qwen result would establish, at best, a useful hidden-state distillation loss.
- The competitive frontier already includes dynamic trajectories, relational alignment, on-policy KD, and low-rank reasoning traces, as documented in [QL4](<C:\Users\devan\OneDrive\Desktop\Projects\AI Moonshots\moonshot-cti-universal-law\research\QUESTION_LOOP_04_BASELINES_2026_07_25.md>).
- The 6.7× same-family GSM8K pilot cannot deliver the manifesto even if it wins by three points.
- The 1,900–3,500-hour ladder in [QL7](<C:\Users\devan\OneDrive\Desktop\Projects\AI Moonshots\moonshot-cti-universal-law\research\QUESTION_LOOP_07_EVIDENCE_LADDER_2026_07_25.md>) is not bureaucratic excess. Each rung blocks a genuinely fatal alternative explanation.

Continuing DG-0 unchanged would be optimizing a method before establishing that its object carries information.

## The replacement primary bet

Pivot from **Dynamic Geometry Compiler** to an **Open Capability File** program:

> **Turn one AI skill into a tiny file that can be installed in many cheap, incompatible models.**

Or, for the gossip-magazine version:

> **“Download a skill, not a giant AI.”**

The scientific hypothesis becomes:

> A teacher’s differential competence can be extracted once into a compact, causally grounded behavioral artifact that installs into multiple student substrates, composes with other skills, and costs less than ordinary distillation.

Raw \(R\), observable connection, perturbation-response kernels, Jacobian sketches, and perhaps holonomy should compete as candidate codecs. **Geometry is no longer the thesis; portable competence is the thesis.**

This distinction matters because “skill patch” alone is already occupied: ICLR 2026’s [GraftLLM](https://openreview.net/forum?id=wJtD28wGV1) packages capabilities into modular SkillPacks and explicitly targets heterogeneous-model fusion. Our paradigm-level differentiator must therefore be stronger:

- The same source artifact works across incompatible students.
- It is not stored in a target model’s parameter coordinates.
- It can be added, subtracted, composed, and relayed.
- It is extracted once and installed offline.
- It wins end-to-end economics against logits, rationales, and parameter deltas.

Similarly, gauge-invariant [representation holonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21653) already exists as a diagnostic. The novelty would have to be causal, transferable competence—not inventing the diagnostic.

## Other manifesto-serving alternatives

The best fallback is **Universal Compute Governor**:

> “A cheap meter tells any AI when more thinking is worth paying for.”

That directly serves democratization through lower inference cost. Gate C is its admission test. It should become primary only after beating confidence, entropy, and margin in both transfer directions and then demonstrating a material cost reduction—not merely a one-point accuracy gain.

A Gate B success could support a **Capability Compass**:

> “Predict which small AI will work before paying to benchmark or train it.”

Useful and potentially economically meaningful, but not yet paradigm-shifting. It needs a prospective cost-of-knowing trial showing saved dollars and avoided bad model choices.

I would not lead with model routing or small-model swarms. They retain online coordination and inference overhead, and the field is already crowded. Capability files offer the cleaner democratization endpoint: a cheap model becomes independently more capable after installation.
