**Archive this repository as an active research program. Do not authorize direction fourteen here.**

One important correction: **W-D2 did not scientifically kill the Atlas under its own protocol.** It killed only the narrow hypothesis that raw local checkpoints exhibit within-family inversions on MKQA. ## 1. What the thirteen kills actually taught

“Thirteen kills” overstates the amount of independent scientific evidence. Several were literature or strategy rejections, not empirical refutations:

| Failure class | Directions | Actual lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Invalid assay or prediction | CTI, equicorrelation, benchmark bridge | A predictor adjacent to its target, dependent on task intercepts, confounded by scale, or reproduced by a null is not a law. |
| Artifact without consequence | AMCL, GAT | Infrastructure, compactness, installability, and geometric resemblance do not establish capability. |
| Failed mechanism acquisition | CSO | Supplying a causal interface does not mean a learner can discover the mechanism. This run failed; extraction in general was not disproved. |
| Novelty failure | ACQ, CCL, PCI, VIL, CIF, PCSI | Renamed classical theory, occupied empirical territory, or a conjunction of familiar ingredients is not a foundational principle. |
| Missing phenomenon | Atlas discovery screen | Raw checkpoint quality scaled monotonically within three families on one multilingual-QA sample. |

The hard constraints are:

1. **The target must be independent of the predictor.** CTI’s strongest fit used per-dataset intercepts, while new-task transfer was weak; its own simple baselines also beat it on predictive error. The surviving conclusion is a within-task geometry diagnostic, not a universal predictor. [STATUS](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/STATUS.md:10>)

2. **Measurement is not intervention.** GAT installed the intended geometry, yet correct geometry was consistently worse than the Haar control and capability remained near chance. Observational resemblance is not functional equivalence. [GAT record](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/STATUS.md:41>)

3. **Acquisition is part of the scientific problem.** You cannot supply states, sockets, decompositions, reset access, or mechanism boundaries and then claim the learner discovered them.

4. **Component competence does not imply composition.** CSO achieved roughly 76% per-register behavior but only 33.9% exact training success and essentially zero length extrapolation. The transition rule remained unlearned. [CSO record](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/STATUS.md:68>)

5. **A novelty audit must precede implementation.** ACQ, CCL, PCI, VIL, CIF, and PCSI were mostly killed by established theory or occupied literature—not by experiments. That means the project repeatedly built or designed before resolving the cheapest intellectual objection. [Direction-selection record](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/STATUS.md:134>)

6. **The ontology cannot be chosen by the benchmark designer.** Synthetic worlds containing the desired quotient, geometry, organ, or interaction advantage cannot validate that object as a general principle.

7. **Full cost and deployment consequence must be primary.** Parameters, representation size, proxy fidelity, and theoretical operation counts do not substitute for fixed-target quality, joules, wall time, acquisition, tuning, verification, and human effort.

8. **No more infrastructure before nontriviality.** A one-day baseline or analytic lemma must establish that a phenomenon exists before protocols, verifiers, runners, and large matrices are built.

What the project has **not** learned:

- that scaling is universally necessary;
- that complete small systems never beat larger systems;
- that compact causal mechanisms do not exist;
- that mechanism extraction is impossible;
- or that training-time structure cannot reduce compute.

Those would be overclaims.

## 2. Atlas adjudication

The W-D2 evidence is real but narrow:

- Nine raw checkpoints, three families, 40 independent query clusters.
- Macro-F1 ranged from 0.040 to 0.254.
- All six adjacent within-family comparisons favored the larger checkpoint in my paired query-cluster bootstrap.
- Gemma-12B versus Qwen-14B was statistically unresolved; attributing that ordering specifically to “training-language coverage” is plausible but **not identifiable** because architecture, data, tokenizer, recipe, quantization, and implementation all changed together. [Gate-A data](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/results/atlas_r2_gate_a_output.json:205>)

But the binding Atlas thesis concerned **complete systems**—RAG, adaptation, verification, cost, and frontier APIs—and its explicit kill criterion applied only **after discovery and eligible prospective confirmations**. [Headline criterion](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/results/codex_scale_inversion_atlas_design_gate_r2.md:399>) [Binding kill condition](</C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/AI Moonshots/moonshot-cti-universal-law/results/codex_scale_inversion_atlas_design_gate_r2.md:425>)

Therefore:

> **W-D2 kills “raw smaller checkpoints beat larger same-family checkpoints on sampled MKQA.” It does not kill the Scale-Inversion Atlas as designed.**

Nevertheless, I would not continue it. Completing the design costs up to 360 GPU-hours and $1,200, while no API comparison, complete-system inversion, usable quality floor, selector, or confirmation evidence exists. Its plausible success is a valuable 5–7/10 systems result—not the 9+ foundational opportunity this project exists to pursue.
